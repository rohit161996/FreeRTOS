# RTOS vs GPOS : Priority inversion

## What is Priority Inversion?
- Priority inversion is an accidental bug in which the Low Priority Task keeps the hold of the Shared Resource which is required by the High Priority Task.

- This scenario inverts the priority of the Low Priority and the High Priority Task.

- Example:
  - Larry (Low Priority) starts his workday early. He needs to staple some papers, so he picks up the office stapler.
  - Harry walks over to use the stapler, but Larry is currently using it. Harry cannot do his urgent work without the stapler. Harry has to sit down and wait for Larry to finish.
  - Mary has a stack of spreadsheets to type up. Typing does not require the stapler. Because Mary is a higher rank than Larry, she tells Larry: "Move out of the way, my work is more important than yours." Mary preempts Larry and takes over the desk to type her spreadsheets.

## Why was Mary able to preempt Lary but Harry was not able to preempt Lary?
- Mary didn't require the stapler, she had all the resources but Harry was blocked due to resource unavailability which was with Larry.

- In RTOS this problem is solved by making Larry a temporary boss(cannot be preempted), to free the resources required by Harry from Larry.

## NOTE:
- Priority inversion effects are in-significant in GPOS, but they should be solved in the RTOS.

## What are the features that a RTOS has but a GPOS doesn't?
1. RTOS has `priority based preemptive scheduling mechanism`, if the higher priority task is in the ready state and is waiting to execute, it will be executed first. The GPOS will `not` go for the `priority based preemptive scheduling mechanism` because it will kill the throughput this way.

2. RTOS application `will have a very short Critical Section` or `will not have a Critical Section` at all which disables the preemption.

3. `Priority inversion is avoided` in the RTOS because the timeliness of the tasks are of atmost priority, they should not be compromised this is why they do not have Critical Section or they have a very short Critical Section to avoid the Priority Inversion Problem.

4. RTOS has bounded Interrupt Latency.

5. RTOS has bounded Scheduling Latency.
